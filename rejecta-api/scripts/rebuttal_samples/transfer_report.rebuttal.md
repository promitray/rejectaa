# Point-by-Point Response to Reviewers
**Target Journal**: Chemical communication

Dear Reviewers,

We thank the Reviewers for the time and care they gave our manuscript. Below we respond to each comment in turn and describe the revisions we propose to make.

*Collaborative revision stance: 2 point(s) answered with a bounded position.*

---

## Response to Reviewer 1

### Comment R1C1 (Bounded Agreement with Counter-Proposal)
> *"This manuscript describes a dual-responsive polymer system, which can be disassembled into small molecules by UV light-based on a benzophenone trigger motif and a thermo responsive reversible linkages. The light-triggering mechanism involves backbone degradation via intra molecular lactone formation. The synthesis of the polymer and the characterization of the structure and the disassembly are properly conducted. However, there are already several examples of on-demand disassembly polymers in the literature. Although the current one is new, it does not add any significant novelty to the field. Therefore, I think that this work is suitable for publication in JACS but rather than in a more specialized journal."*

**Author Response:**
We appreciate the reviewer's insights regarding the novelty of our dual-responsive polymer system. We understand that the field of on-demand disassembly polymers is well-established, and while our work introduces a new system, we recognize the reviewer's perspective on its contribution to the broader field. We have decided to submit our manuscript to Macromolecules, a journal that aligns well with the specialized nature of our work and its intended audience. We believe this venue will effectively reach polymer chemists and materials scientists who can benefit from our findings.

**Action in Revised Manuscript:**
Propose to sharpen the statement of contribution in the Introduction to better highlight the aspects of our polymer system within the specialized field.

## Response to Reviewer 2

### Comment R2C1 (Bounded Agreement with Counter-Proposal)
> *"The manuscript by Multu and Barner-Kowollik describes some very nice analytical work on the degradation mechanisms of a dually-responsive (heat and light) polymer system. The system is a practical extension of known reactivities for the most part, and should contribute to the ever-growing suite of functional material building blocks available to polymer chemists and materials scientists. There are concerns regarding the level of impact and novelty that give me reservations about publication in JACS. For example: the system can be described as an incremental modification/improvement to either dynamic polymers or "chain shattering" polymers, but the ensemble doesn't really offer any demonstrable advantages in the field."*

**Author Response:**
We thank Reviewer 2 for recognizing the analytical work on the degradation mechanisms of our dually-responsive polymer system. We acknowledge the reviewer's concerns regarding the impact and novelty of our work. Our study aims to expand the suite of functional material building blocks by introducing a system that integrates known reactivities in a novel configuration. In response to the reviewer's suggestion, we have decided to submit our manuscript to Macromolecules, which we believe is a more appropriate venue for our work.

**Action in Revised Manuscript:**
Propose to sharpen the statement of contribution in the Introduction to better articulate the insights offered by the dual-responsive polymer system.
